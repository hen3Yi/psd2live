# Skeleton and posing

[Documentation](../../README.md) · [中文](../../zh/guide/SKELETON.md) · [Quick reference](USER_GUIDE.md) · [Physics](PHYSICS.md)

The skeleton bakes the body, limbs, tail and wings into structures Cubism runs directly: the upper and lower body are **warp deformers** at the end of the body deformer chain, **rotation deformers** turn whole parts such as the limbs, and meshes that cross a joint bend smoothly through **corrective keyforms**. The exported moc3/cmo3 does not depend on this program and stays editable in Cubism Editor.

## Workflow

Skeleton work happens in the **Skeleton** tab, which sits in the same panel as the **Hierarchy** as a sibling tab (older layouts gain the tab automatically; it can also be opened or closed on its own from the Windows menu). The tree lists every bone by hierarchy, with the drawables bound to it below, marked in the same color and shown by layer name (the name given when splitting); clicking a drawable's name selects its layer in the Hierarchy, or, while editing the skeleton, the bone it belongs to.

**The skeleton is a selection object of its own**, alongside drawables and deformers. Posing and skeleton editing both happen in the canvas's **Skeleton** mode (mode menu, `Alt+5` by default); Deform, Edit and the other modes do not act on the skeleton:

| Mode | Skeleton |
| --- | --- |
| Object | Bones show faintly on the canvas; clicking a bone (or a bone row in the **Skeleton** tab) selects the skeleton, and selecting a drawable or deformer deselects it. The **Skeleton** toggle in the view options hides this bone layer; hidden bones cannot be clicked on the canvas but can still be selected from the tab |
| Skeleton | The toolbar holds two tools, **Pose Bones** (`Shift+W` by default) and **Edit Bones** (`Alt+W` by default); either key enters Skeleton mode from any mode. On entry, Pose Bones is used when the skeleton is enabled, Edit Bones otherwise; selecting a drawable or deformer in Skeleton mode returns to Object mode |

1. **Create / edit the skeleton**: choose the **Edit Bones** tool in Skeleton mode, or in Object mode right-click a drawable and choose **Create skeleton** / **Edit skeleton**. When the project has no skeleton yet, entering Skeleton mode does not generate one; a **Create skeleton** button appears in the middle of the canvas, and clicking it (or the button of the same name in the **Skeleton** tab) creates it. The red trash can at the tab's top right, **Delete skeleton**, removes the whole skeleton (one history entry, undoable), after which a new one can be created. While editing, each bound drawable is filled semi-transparently on the canvas in its bone's color, the selected bone's drawables more strongly. Creating infers a skeleton automatically:
   - **Binding by semantics**: by layer tag and side — arms bind handwear on the same side, legs bind legwear, feet bind footwear, and the tail and wings bind their own layers. Toggle and switch variant layers are bound too but do not take part in estimating bone positions.
   - **Placement by body build**:
     - Shoulder joints sit below the torso's shoulder line, inside the torso edge; hip joints sit below the hip line (placed even when a skirt covers them).
     - A bent elbow/knee sits inside the outer corner of the bend; straight limbs are placed by proportion: upper arm : forearm : hand ≈ 1 : 0.85 : 0.55, thigh ≈ shin.
     - The wrist is placed by the forearm-to-hand proportion; with shoes, the ankle sits at the shoe opening and the toe at the shoe's farthest end.
     - The tail is split into 3–4 segments along the medial axis inside its mesh, forming a flexible chain (see "Flexible chains" below). A fairly straight axis is split evenly; where it turns clearly, joints go at the turns so that each bone's chord lies closest to the axis.
   - Drag joint endpoints to adjust positions; click an ArtMesh to bind it to the current bone.
   - In the **Edit** sub-tool, Shift-click adds or removes from the selection, dragging empty space box-selects, and dragging a bone body moves the whole selection. The **Tool Details** panel offers select all, select chain, rotate and scale; rotation and scaling pivot about the center of the selection's endpoints and can include descendants. Externally connected endpoints follow; disconnected endpoints stay put.
   - The **Tool Details** panel also offers duplicate, mirror, subdivide and dissolve. Duplicates bind no meshes by default, with an option to transfer the bindings to the copy; the include-descendants option controls whether the whole chain is copied. Mirroring takes an X axis, flips side and direction automatically, and shows how many opposite-side meshes match unambiguously; ambiguous layers stay unbound. With symmetric editing on, joint moves and selection transforms of mirrored pairs are linked.
   - Subdivide splits the current limb bone into 2–16 segments, keeping the first segment's ID and bindings, with child bones attached to the last segment; dissolve merges the currently connected child bone into its unbranched limb parent and moves its bindings over. The built-in body/head bones are not subdivided or dissolved; their copies become custom bones. Subdivide/dissolve rebake from the rest skeleton and break the affected mirror pairs.
   - **Sub-tools**: under **Edit Bones** the left toolbar lists **Edit / New Bone / Extrude / Bind / Weights** (`1`–`5`, also switchable from the canvas context menu). Edit moves joints and binds meshes; New Bone drags from a start point to an end point to create an independent custom bone with no parent; Extrude drags from the selected bone's tip, or clicks empty space, to create a child bone and selects the new bone so you can keep extending. A preview shows while dragging and the bone is created on release; nothing is created for a length under 4 screen pixels. New bones bind no meshes by default; bind them after switching back to **Edit**. **Add bone** at the top of the tree keeps the shortcut that extends 60 pixels downward by default, and selects the new bone.
   - Bound meshes get automatic weights across the **whole limb**, so a limb's meshes only need binding to one of its bones.
   - **Flexible**: check **Flexible (smooth curve)** in the bone panel and the selected bone becomes a link of a flexible chain. Soft, long parts such as tails, fins and fingers use it to bend into one smooth curve instead of folding at the joints. A flexible bone with several flexible children is a branch point, for example the upper and lower lobes of a fish tail; branch points can only be at joints, so subdivide first to branch mid-bone.
   - **Straighten chain**: select a bone on a flexible chain and click **Straighten chain**. The chain turns into a straight line link by link from the root, and the drawing unrolls along it (only mesh vertices move; texture and UVs are unchanged). The rest pose becomes straight, the drawing's bend is saved as the pose "<bone name> drawn", and each joint's limits widen automatically to include it. From there, keyforms and physics can start from the straight state. Branches stay spread and each is straightened separately. **Bend back to drawing** restores the bones and meshes to the drawing.
   - Pick a bone in the tree, then click an ArtMesh on the canvas to bind it; the "×" at the end of a drawable row unbinds it. Tail and wings can be added or removed with the checkboxes at the top of the tab.
   - For batch work, switch to the **Bind** sub-tool, select the target bone in the tree, then click or box-select meshes, or check them in the **Tool Details** list. Shift-box adds meshes. The canvas highlights the pending meshes and **Tool Details** shows their count and the target bone; **Bind selected** or **Unbind selected** applies to all of them.
   - The **Weights** sub-tool selects a bound limb mesh, with a bone of the same limb selected in the tree; click/drag to add, subtract, replace or smooth weights, blue to orange showing the selected bone's influence. The paint mode (add / subtract / replace / smooth), radius, strength and replace value are set in the tool options bar at the canvas's bottom left (and in the context menu). The current angle skinning keeps one bone, or two adjacent bones, per vertex, and painting normalizes automatically.
   - Weight cleanup takes 1–2 influences and a low-weight cutoff, and detects and fixes empty values, invalid references and unnormalized rows; automatic weights can also be restored. Copy / mirror weights first picks a source, a target, the interpolated / nearest-vertex / same-topology method and the bone mapping, and shows the matched and unmatched counts before applying; unmatched vertices keep automatic weights. Nearest vertex has a distance limit and mirroring takes an axis; same topology requires identical vertex counts and triangle indices.
   - Hand-painted weights are saved with the project and used for the preview and export baking. Weights are stored by rest position and triangle samples, and vertices added by mesh densification inherit them by interpolation; when a bone is subdivided, hand-weighted meshes it influences return to automatic weights so the new segments are redistributed.
   - With a bone selected, the bottom of the tab sets the **Joint band** (how wide the smooth transition on either side of the joint is; **Auto** restores the default) and the **Min** / **Max** angle; the angle range is the range of that bone's parameter.
   - In the bone settings, the name saves on Enter or when the field loses focus; bone ID and parameter stay unchanged. The parent drop-down changes or clears the parent bone, keeping coordinates by default; turning on **Connect to parent tip** moves the whole subtree to the parent's tip. Turning it off keeps the parent and position, and coincident endpoints no longer move together. A bone and its descendants cannot be set as its parent.
   - After editing the skeleton, **Parameter sampling** adjusts the tolerance (pixels), minimum angle step and per-mesh keyform limit. The model rebuilds while sliding and shows the total number of parameter points actually generated; each bone in the tree shows its parameter's point count. During the calculation **Calculating parameter points…** shows; the settings are saved with the project, and **Reset** restores the defaults.
   - **Leaving the edit saves it**: switching to the **Pose Bones** tool, switching to another mode, selecting another layer or clicking **Done** writes the changes as one history entry and rebuilds the model. After **Done**, an enabled skeleton switches to the **Pose Bones** tool for posing; a disabled one returns to the mode used before entering Skeleton mode. Only **Cancel** discards the changes. **Done** and **Cancel** are in the mode bar, at the bottom of the **Skeleton** tab and in the canvas context menu.
   - While editing, **Disable** at the bottom of the tab only turns the skeleton off; bones and bindings are kept, and **Enable** in the tab restores it.
2. **Pose**: choose the **Pose Bones** tool in Skeleton mode; with the skeleton enabled, it is the default tool on entering Skeleton mode.
   - Drag a bone body: FK, turning only that bone.
   - The left toolbar (below the pose tool) or the canvas context menu chooses **Auto / FK / IK** (`1`–`3`). Auto keeps FK for body drags and IK for tip or Shift drags; explicit FK always turns only the current bone, and explicit IK always solves the chain.
   - With a bone selected, the IK settings in **Tool Details** set that tip's chain length (1–32 bones), iterations, pixel tolerance and bend direction; each joint still obeys its own angle limits. After **Pin selected tip**, changing parent parameters keeps solving; dragging the canvas cross or typing target coordinates moves it, and it can be enabled, disabled and released. Unreachable or conflicting targets show the remaining error.
   - IK targets and settings are saved in the project. Exported models keep the parameter ranges and motions keep the solved angles; exports contain no runtime pinned targets or IK constraints. Saving / inserting a named pose writes the solved result into a motion.
   - The **Tool Details** of the Pose Bones tool saves the current model parameters under a name (the same name replaces it); a saved pose can be selected and applied or deleted. Applying skips removed parameters and clamps to the current parameter ranges. Open the animation editor and pick a snapshot in the **Insert saved pose** drop-down to insert every still-existing parameter at the playhead, creating missing curves. Existing keys keep their interpolation and handles.
   - Drag a bone's tip, or drag with `Shift` held: IK, turning the bone together with up to two levels of parent bones toward the pointer, each joint obeying its angle limits.
   - The default angle ranges are wide (for example upper arm -90°~150°, forearm ±150°), so drags are not stopped midway; to limit a joint, lower its Min/Max angle while editing the skeleton.
   - Posing only changes parameter values, not meshes, and creates no history entries. **Reset Pose** in the mode bar and canvas context menu zeroes all bone and pose parameters; **Show skin weights** in the display rail at the bottom right colors each vertex by bone color, with colors in the joint bands mixing the two bones' colors.
3. **Motions**: the Animation panel shows the motions the current skeleton can play. The **Motions** group in **Model presets** has only two switches, **Basic motions** (idle, blink, nod, shake) and **Skeleton motions**; switched off, the whole group leaves the Animation panel, the animation editor, the preview and the export, with each motion's switch and advanced settings kept, and switching it back on restores them; single motions are enabled row by row in the Animation panel. When motions are checked at export, the matching `motion3.json` files are written and registered in model3's motion group of the same name. Each preset consists of the **poses** in the table below plus the standard head and body parameters. Poses come in two kinds: **pose parameters** in the model (crouch, weight shift, knees in, hop, tail swing, wing flap), whose effect is baked into the model, the motion only deciding when and how far; and **gestures** (arm swing, arms in/out, raise hand, wave swing, single-leg kick) that have no parameter in the model and are converted into curves of the bone angle parameters when the motion is written, added to the values the motion itself writes on the same bone parameters. The upper arm turn that comes with the leg poses is a gesture too. The leg poses (crouch, weight shift, knees in) only stack; a motion uses one of them and keeps the others at 0; hop needs no landing and can stack on top of them. Poses the skeleton cannot play are left out.

   The preset motions are not fixed-angle templates: generation first reads each arm's direction and length, the head-to-body ratio and the face position from the drawing's rest skeleton, then solves the poses within a comfortable human range (shoulder up to about 115°, no hyperextended elbow, wrist up to 35°); for example, when waving the hand stops beside the face at about eye height without covering it, and when shy both hands come together in front of the body. In time they carry anticipation, ease in and out, staggered lag down the chain, and overshoot and settle, with the rest of the body (weight, body lean, head) following; chibi builds move with more bounce. Keys sit only on the beats, with Bézier curves between them.

| Motion | Condition | Poses driven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idle (12 s loop) | Always | Relaxed like a standing person: three breaths of different depth and length, inhaling fast and exhaling slowly, lifting the shoulders and opening the chest, the body nodding slightly behind and the head a little later still; the weight shift is the sum of two components with 6 s and 4 s periods and does not repeat within the loop. The hips lead the weight shift, the upper body and body turn following in turn, the head tilting slightly the other way to keep the gaze level, turning last and drifting slowly on its own; the arms hang naturally, upper arm, forearm and hand swinging 2~4° with staggered lag, left and right not fully in sync; the tail sways slowly and the wings follow the breathing. Without a skeleton only the body and head parts remain. Curves have keys only at turning points |
| TailSwing (3 s) | A tail with bound meshes | Tail swing, three back-and-forths of 1 s each by default |
| Crouch (2.2 s) | Leg bones | Rises slightly first, then crouches, the body leaning forward and the head lifting to look ahead, the arms drawing in front a little later; a slight bounce at the bottom, leaning back a little on rising before settling. Weight shift stays at 0 |
| WeightShift (3 s) | Leg bones | A small push the other way first, then the weight moves to one side, settles and comes back; the body and head follow with lag, the arms swinging a beat late by inertia. Crouch stays at 0 |
| Shy (3 s) | Leg bones or arms with bound meshes | Bashful fidgeting: a glance up first, then the head lowers and tilts, the gaze looking away; knees in, both hands together in front of the body rubbing slightly, the body twisting slightly left and right |
| Wave (2.8 s) | Arms with bound meshes (right arm first) | Wave: the elbow bends and the hand rises along an arc to beside the face, swinging three and a half times about the elbow, weaker each time, the wrist a beat late; the weight moves to the other side, the body leans slightly the opposite way and the head tilts toward the hand |
| HeadTilt (2.2 s) | Leg bones or arms with bound meshes | Head tilt: a small move the other way first, then a large tilt with a slight overshoot and a one-beat hold; the body and weight lean the same way a little later, both hands drawing in slightly |
| Cheer (2.2 s) | Leg bones or arms with bound meshes | Happy cheer: crouch then jump, take-off and landing with speed, knees bending to cushion the landing, the second jump smaller; both arms fling up beside the head on the first jump, rising and falling with the landing, the elbows bending first as they come down; head up, tail and wings swinging fast |
| LegKick (2.6 s) | Legs with bound meshes | Single-leg kick: the weight moves to the left foot, the right foot kicks up behind and sways gently, both arms open for balance, head tilted |
| Sway (3 s) | Leg bones or arms with bound meshes | Happy sway: a small push, then two side-to-side sways dying down, the parts following with staggered lag, the arms swinging by inertia |
| IdleCute (12 s loop) | Leg bones | Girlish idle: on top of Idle, holds knees in at 0.3 and 30% of the hands-together-in-front pose, the arms still swinging relaxed on that pose, without the looping weight shift. Exported to the Idle motion group as a second idle motion beside the plain Idle; the preview plays one round |

Expanding a preset in the Animation panel shows its advanced settings; changes apply to the preview, the animation editor and the export at once, are saved with the project and can be undone:

| Motion | Advanced settings |
| --- | --- |
| Idle | Sway (weight, body, arms, tail), breathing (breath, body rise, wing flap), head (the three head angles), speed, blink switch |
| IdleCute | As Idle, plus the draw-in amount (the ratio of knees in and hands together, default 0.3) |
| Blink | Closure, speed, double blink |
| Nod / Shake | Amplitude, speed, count (each a little weaker than the one before) |
| TailSwing | Amplitude, speed, count |
| Crouch / Cheer | Crouch depth or strength, speed |
| Other skeleton presets | Amplitude, speed |

Amplitude scales one-shot motions about the starting rest value and looping motions about the curve mean; eye opening does not change with amplitude. Speed scales both key times and duration. After a preset's keys are changed in the animation editor, the preset becomes a manual override and its advanced settings stop applying; **Restore Default** clears both the override and the settings. **Delete** in the context menu removes the preset from the list and the export; it comes back from **Restore deleted presets** in the **New Motion** menu. The play button on the panel shares one playback state with the animation editor: clicking it opens the motion in the editor and plays it, and clicking again or pausing in the editor stays in sync.

When physics is exported, the tail joints from the second segment on and the wings are handed to physics. If every bone a pose involves is driven by physics (for example wing flap), Idle no longer writes that pose, so the two do not conflict. Physics writes the bone parameters, while tail swing and wing flap are blend shapes stacked on top, so both can act at once.

## Tools at a glance

Skeleton mode has two main tools, **Pose Bones** and **Edit Bones**. Edit Bones has 5 sub-tools: Edit, New Bone, Extrude, Bind and Weights; Pose Bones has Auto / FK / IK. Sub-tools are listed under their main tool in the toolbar and share one draft when switching; finishing or leaving the edit saves it as one history entry, and canceling discards the draft.

## Generated structure

| Object | ID | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Torso bend | `DeformSkelTorso` (warp deformer) | Sits under the breath deformer and takes over all of its former children (torso, clothing, arms, wings, tail, head rotation). Uses the upper and lower body parameters as axes: above the waistline follows the upper body and below it the lower body, both turning about the waist, bending smoothly within the waist band. The legs are not in it: when the lower body turns, the skirt and tail turn with it while the feet stay on the ground |
| Bone rotation deformers | `DeformSkel_<bone ID>` | Generated only for limbs, tail and wings. The pivot is at the bone's head (the joint at the parent's tip) and the handle points along the bone (Cubism's handle points up at 0° with clockwise positive, so a bone pointing straight down has `baseAngle` 180°, and nested deformers record only the angle relative to the parent bone); they nest by bone hierarchy. Arms, wings and tail sit under the torso bend, legs under the leg warp (`DeformLegs`). Arms and wings directly under a body deformer also scale with perspective about Body Y as the upper body leans forward or back (a warp's scaling does not reach rotation deformers). Bones with no mesh or child deformer under them get no deformer; joints inside one mesh (with no mesh of their own, only connecting to the next segment) get none either and merge into the next segment's deformer |
| Glue | `GlueSkel__<meshA>__<meshB>` | When one limb is split into several meshes, coincident vertices on the seams are welded automatically |
| Bone parameters | `ParamArmLA`, `ParamArmLB`, `ParamHandL`, `ParamLegLA`, `ParamLegLB`, `ParamFootL`, `ParamTail1..n`, `ParamWingL` and so on | In degrees; positive means swinging away from the body, the same on both sides. Limb bone parameters that can be added are blend shape parameters, the rest normal parameters (see "Blend shape bones") |
| Crouch | `ParamSkelCrouch` (0..1, blend shape) | The body sinks by 10% of leg length, knees toward the viewer and slightly outward, feet in place |
| Weight shift | `ParamSkelWeight` (-1..1, blend shape) | The hips shift sideways and tilt slightly, sinking when needed so both feet still reach the ground; the upper body rights itself the other way; during the motion the upper arms follow behind (gesture) |
| Tail swing | `ParamSkelTailSwing` (-1..1, blend shape) | The tail curls to one side segment by segment, turning more toward the tip |
| Wing flap | `ParamSkelWingFlap` (-1..1, blend shape) | Both wings flap symmetrically |
| Knees in | `ParamSkelKneesIn` (0..1, blend shape) | The body sinks slightly, the knees turn inward, feet in place; during the motion the upper arms draw in slightly (gesture) |
| Hop | `ParamSkelHop` (0..1, blend shape) | The body and leg warps move up together and the legs leave the ground; stacked on crouch it is a bent-knee jump |

The leg poses act on the body warp (hip motion) and the leg warp (legs bending by two-bone IK with the soles fixed), so they work too when the legs are drawn on one layer and the leg bones have no bound mesh; when the legs are split and bound to leg bones, the leg bones' rotation deformers additionally turn by IK.

The gestures below have no parameter in the model and play through the bone angle parameters. The preset motions solve their arm poses for the body build; only LegKick's single-leg kick still uses the gesture here:

| Gesture | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Arm swing | Left and right arms swing opposite each other, the amplitude decreasing toward the far joints |
| Arms in/out | -1 draws both hands together in front of the body (upper arm, forearm and hand all turning toward the body); +1 opens the arms and raises them |
| Raise hand (right) | Turns only the character's right arm: elbow raised to shoulder height, forearm upright, beside the face |
| Wave swing | Right forearm and hand swing side to side, used on top of the raised hand |
| Single-leg kick | +1 the character's left leg, -1 the right: the thigh draws in slightly, the shin flips outward and the toes follow; can stack on the weight shift |

## Algorithm

- **Deformer hierarchy**: a rotation deformer under a warp deformer inherits only the pivot position and angle; the upper warp's non-rigid deformation does not pass down. A warp deformer under a warp deformer transforms only the child deformer's control points. So the body bones do not use rotation deformers; they are fine-grid warp deformers at the end of the body chain: the bending of Body X/Y, Body Z and breathing passes all the way to the torso mesh, and the shoulder and head pivots move with it; the fine waist bend acts directly on the meshes and limbs without being resampled by coarse-grid upper deformers. A warp deformer is the identity at rest, so inserting it moves nothing and does not change the children's coordinates. With the skeleton enabled, the shoulder line and waistline of breathing and Body Z come from the upper body bone, so breathing lifts only above the waist and Body Z tilts about the upper body bone's waist; the hip, knee and ankle of the leg warp come from the leg bones. For the body deformer structure see the [deformer and parameter reference](../spec/DEFORMER_AND_PARAMETER_SPEC.md).
  ```
  DeformBodyXY (Body X/Y: torso turn; the body shifts, sinks and rises with the hips; hip motion of the leg poses)
   └ DeformBodyLean (body lean forward/back)
       └ DeformBodyZBreath (Body Z, breathing)
           └ DeformSkelTorso (upper body + lower body)
               ├ torso, clothing, skirt and other meshes
               ├ rotation deformers of arms, wings and tail
               └ DeformHeadRotation → head
  DeformLegs (legs under Body X/Y and the leg poses, soles fixed)
   ├ unbound leg meshes
   └ leg bone rotation deformers
       └ DeformSkel_<bone ID>Stance (stance under Body X/Y) → leg meshes bound to this bone
  ```
- **Waist bend**: both the upper and lower body pivot about the waist (the bone's head). The distance to the waist along the bone direction transitions by smoothstep within the **Joint band**; outside the band the turn is fully rigid. The default half-width is 25% of the bone length, at most 45%, adjustable by selecting the upper/lower body while editing the skeleton.
- **Rigid parts**: rotation deformers interpolate angles, not vertex positions, so at any parameter value the limb is an exact rigid rotation and does not shorten between keys; when several joints turn at once, keyforms do not need to be multiplied either.
- **Weights**: each vertex follows at most the two bones on either side of a joint. The bone nearest the vertex is its primary bone; within the joint bands at either end of the primary bone, it transitions by smoothstep on the signed distance to the joint's angle bisector. The joint band's default half-width is 35% of the shorter of the two bones, at most 45%, so the bands at the two ends of one bone never overlap.
- **Angle blending**: vertices in a joint band rotate about the joint by "weight × relative angle", instead of blending linearly between two rigid positions (LBS). The vertex's distance to the joint stays the same, so the limb keeps its width when bending and the inside of the elbow does not collapse.
- **Where meshes attach**:
  - Each mesh preferably attaches under the rotation deformer of **the joint it covers that is closest to the body**. The connection to the previous segment is then carried directly by the deformer, and turning far bones does not leave the connection relying on keyform interpolation to compensate. Only when a mesh covers no joint is its attachment bone chosen by weights and parameter coupling. The parts of a mesh that reach onto other bones are written into the mesh's own keyforms by those bones' angles.
  - **Unsplit** (one mesh covering the whole limb): if the mesh contains shoulder, elbow and wrist, it attaches under the upper arm, and the shoulder connection follows the upper arm exactly; elbow and wrist bend in the mesh keyforms. If stockings are drawn from mid-thigh to the ankle, covering the knee but not the hip, the mesh attaches under the shin; the thigh deformer above still acts as the hip pivot carrying the whole leg. Bones below with no mesh (for example the foot) get no deformer.
  - **Split** (one mesh per segment): each segment attaches under its own bone's rotation deformer. Vertices in the joint band are still skinned by position, so the two segments deform identically where they overlap and the seam does not open; coincident vertices on the seam are additionally glued automatically.
  - **Intermediate joints** (for example thigh and shin in one mesh attached under the thigh, the foot in another): each mesh has at most one rotation deformer, and the shin gets none of its own. The foot's rotation deformer attaches directly under the thigh and takes the shin parameter as its own keyform axis: angles add directly, the pivot is keyed along the arc around the knee, and the key spacing follows the distance to the knee so that the pivot strays from the arc by no more than 1px, matching the mesh's precision. When the shin is a blend shape parameter, this part is written as a blend shape too.
- **Corrective keyforms**: keyforms use only the relevant joints' parameters as axes. Each axis is divided evenly on each side of 0, with the fewest keys for which linear interpolation between two keys strays from the true arc by no more than the set tolerance; the default tolerance is 1px and the minimum angle step 7.5°. Parts close to a joint (for example where only the joint band is affected) get sparse keys; only long rigid stretches beyond the attachment bone need dense keys. A single mesh's keyform total is capped at 600 by default, and above that keys are reduced starting from the axis with the most keys. Every keyform is computed from the deformer transforms the runtime actually builds, so the rigid parts land exactly where the deformers put them; for full precision, split the limb into segments.
- **Blend shape bones**: a keyform grid stores one shape per combination of axes, so a mesh across the elbow and wrist stores "forearm keys × hand keys" keyforms. If no vertex is influenced by both bones at once, the two joints' bends simply add, and a blend shape needs only "forearm keys + hand keys" shapes. When the export target is Cubism 5.0 or later, bone parameters that meet this are blend shape parameters: the rotation deformer keeps a single rest shape and the angle is interpolated linearly by blend shapes between the min, 0 and max keys, still an exact rigid rotation; the mesh's corrections on that bone become blend shapes too. A parameter has one kind for the whole model, so if anywhere needs multiplication (a vertex influenced by two bones at once, or two consecutive intermediate joints folded into one rotation deformer), the parameter stays a normal parameter. Tail and wings are read and written by physics and are always normal parameters. The attachment bone is decided first by joint coverage to keep connections stable; only when a mesh covers no joint is the bone that keeps the joints independent preferred.

- **Joint shape templates**: the outer shape of knees and elbows comes from the pose references of the MHR v1.0.1 human model (`src/main/resources/skinning/mhr-joints.tsv`, extracted by `tools/research/extract_mhr_joint_templates.py`, license in `licenses/MHR.txt`): 25 axial cross-sections and 11 angles per joint, normalized by the half-width of the straight joint. `SkeletonJointTemplates` fits the width from the intersections of the mesh boundary with the joint cross-sections and obtains a local residual relative to linear skinning, applied only within about three half-widths on either side of the joint with a smooth falloff; hand weights are not recomputed. The inner side of every connected joint whose cross-section can be fitted (including wrists, ankles and custom chains) folds onto the bend's angle bisector by the same rule; the inner blend band may overlap and flip, while the outer side and faces not taking part in the fold still have their triangle orientation checked. The reference is a 2D projection of a single neutral body build on the bend plane, so chains with a rest angle above 25°, or with an unclosed or overly wide cross-section, keep the base ARAP on the outer side. New bones' joint bands default to 16 axial intervals, adjustable with **Joint mesh density** in the bone sampling panel (skeleton JSON version 10 saves `sampling.jointMeshSegments`; older files default to 4).
- **Joint densification**: bound meshes get **Joint mesh density** + 1 rows of vertices inserted along the angle bisector within each joint band (16 intervals, 17 rows by default; old skeletons without the setting use 4 intervals, 5 rows). Vertices are always inserted where the rows meet the outline, so the outline can bend into an arc. New vertices are affine combinations of the original ones, so nothing on screen moves, and existing keyforms and Glue are remapped automatically. Repeated baking adds no more vertices.
- **Flexible chains**: the bones are the centerline's control polygon, like a path deformer's handles: the centerline starts at the root bone's head, runs along the bones, rounds a corner at each joint and ends at the last bone's tail. Each round corner takes the same distance before and after the joint (half the shorter bone on either side) and joins them with a rational quadratic Bézier (the middle control point is the joint, weight cos(turn/2)): with equal sides it is exactly a circular arc, and the turn is spread evenly through the corner instead of concentrated at the joint. So however the bones are divided, the bend stays smooth: short bones meet in tight small corners, long bones are straight mid-bone, the whole curve is tangent-continuous and never kinks backward; the tail tip is always at the bones' tail tip and the mesh is never shorter than the bones. At a branch, the stem runs straight to the branch point and each lobe starts turning from there, so turning one lobe does not affect the stem or the other lobe; near the branch point, a lobe's mesh transitions smoothly from "turning rigidly with the stem's end" to "following the lobe's curve" over half a lobe bone's length, so the inner corner does not tear.
  - **Binding by connectivity only**: samples are taken along each bone (only samples inside the drawing's mesh; bone parts sticking out of the drawing take no part), and the vertex nearest each sample is a seed; a multi-source shortest path along mesh edges then assigns each vertex to the bone of the seed nearest by mesh distance. Two tail sections curled against each other but not connected in the mesh do not pull each other. The arc length is the vertex's nearest point on the drawing's curve, searched only within its own bone and half a bone before and after; the vertex records its arc length and lateral offset from the curve, and then turns as a whole with the curve's local frame, regardless of how far it is from the centerline. Vertices on a flexible chain are fully determined by the curve and no longer go through joint templates and ARAP; between a rigid parent bone (for example a palm) and the flexible root bone the ordinary joint band transition still applies.
  - **Arc length**: arc length is computed from the bones' rest lengths, each curve segment mapped proportionally to the length of the bone it stands for, so however the bones bend, vertices keep their relative position on their bone.
  - **Baking**: the chain's root is still an exact rotation deformer. When a mesh is influenced by only one or two joints, the bend is written into the mesh's corrective keyforms, with the key count fitted to the same tolerance as rigid chains. When a whole mesh is on the flexible chain and influenced by three or more joints (long tails, forked fish tails), it switches to one nested warp deformer per joint (`DeformSkel_<bone ID>Flex`): a keyform grid would store the product of all joints' key counts, and with six joints under the 600 limit each joint would get only the three keys -90°, 0 and 90°, so in between the mesh interpolates linearly between two keys' shapes and visibly shortens; nested warp deformers each use only their own parameter as axis, so key counts add and are filled to the 1px tolerance. Each deformer turns the part after its round corner about the joint; each point inside the corner moves rigidly with the curve point and tangent at the same fraction of the corner's arc length, so the corner closes toward the bend and the part before the joint does not swing out the other way (turning purely by weight about the joint swings the part before the joint the opposite way, and several segments bending together make a wave). This is exactly what the curve looks like on the bent bones, so wherever the deformers turn, the mesh lands on the bones' curve at that moment and the tail tip matches the bones. At a branch, the lobe turns about the branch point by weight from the branch point to the lobe's midpoint. Grid points take the arc length of the nearest mesh vertex (from the connectivity-only binding), and the grid spacing is refined by the narrowest gap between unconnected neighboring parts (up to 64×64); where an inner deformer turns the grid into empty space of the outer grid, the empty space takes the weight due to the part turned there. Parameters, physics, tail swing and the other poses are unchanged; tail swing is added to each deformer as a blend shape. Flexible bone parameters are not blend shapes.
  - **Straightening**: before straightening, the chain's stem (root bone to branch point or tail tip) is first fitted to the drawing's centerline: every 6px along the chain the middle of the drawing's outline is sampled (end cuts whose cross-section is under 60% of the width before and after do not count), and the joints are moved by least squares so the centerline curve passes through these points; the root bone's head stays put, each joint has a weak pull back to its original position, and lobes after a branch move with the branch point. When the centerline does not follow the drawing, every deviation stays on either side of the straight line after unrolling, as a wave; with joints drawn on the drawing's centerline, the curve also cuts into the inside of turns. After fitting comes straightening: each bone records `drawnBend`, how much more the drawing bends at that bone's head than the bone's rest geometry. Binding happens on the drawing's geometry (the rest skeleton bent back by `drawnBend`), and the rest mesh is placed on the rest curve. Each joint takes `drawnBend` as an extra key, so the drawing's pose is restored exactly in the keyforms. Skeleton JSON version 11 saves `flex` and `drawnBend`; when reading skeletons older than version 11, tail chains become flexible chains automatically. Meshes on nested warp deformers are placed on the rest curve too; when the deformers turn to the drawing's bend, the mesh lands back on the drawing's curve, so the drawing's pose is restored within the grid's precision.
  - **Meshes attached elsewhere**: meshes that history attached to a non-bone deformer (or wrapped in their own warp) skip baking and are skinned after history replay; they also bend along the flexible chain's curve. When straightening, this step cannot move the mesh's rest vertices, so the displacement "from the drawing unrolled to straight" is written into each of its keyforms (including the rest key), and blend shapes are measured from the rest key.
- **Pose parameters and gestures**: a mesh across a joint carries the part on the joint's other side in its own shapes, and where that part lands depends on the joint's **total angle**. Blend shapes can only add the displacements each parameter causes alone, and the sum of displacements from turning one joint twice is not the displacement of turning it once by the total angle: when a pose stacks on a bone angle, or two poses turn the same joint, the limb strays from the bone, by up to a hundred pixels or more at large angles. So limb joints are turned only by their own bone parameters: poses that turn only limb joints are gestures, converted by the motion into bone angles for playback. Each track of a motion is a cubic curve in time between its key points (Bézier handles fixed at 1/3 of the time), and poses are linear between neighboring keys, so the bone curve between these moments is cubic too: the conversion takes points at each key point and at the moments the pose passes its own key values, with the slopes on either side, so the bone curve matches the pose exactly. The model keeps only the poses bone parameters cannot replace: the hip motion of the leg poses (with IK keeping both feet on the ground) and the upper body righting itself, plus the tail and wings driven by physics. They stack as blend shapes on the relevant objects: the body and leg warps (hip motion and leg bending), the torso bend (upper body righting the other way), bone rotation deformers (leg IK angles, tail and wing angles), and skinned meshes (bending in the joint bands). Leg poses stacked with manually turned leg angles such as the ankle still have the error above (for example a full crouch with the ankle turned 40° puts the toes off by about 10px); among the preset motions only LegKick stacks this way, off by about 2.5px.
- **Leg posture**: crouch and weight shift are solved from the hip position at every key value. In the leg warp each leg bends by two-bone IK over hip, knee and ankle: the leg keeps the drawing's sideways bend, the extra bend goes toward the viewer so that from the front the thigh shortens instead of swinging out, and then turns inward or outward per pose; the sole row is fixed. When the hip shift keeps a leg from reaching the drawing's straightness, the hips sink automatically until both feet reach. Leg bones with bound meshes solve their rotation by two-bone IK from the hip position at runtime, the knee bending outward (inward with knees in) and the sole kept level. Body X/Y is carried by a stance warp under the leg bone: rotation deformers do not pass down the leg warp's bending, so each leg bone carrying a mesh has a warp under it with Body X/Y as axes (3 keys each), whose control points at each key value are solved back from the bone positions at runtime so the mesh lands where the leg warp puts it — hips moving with the body, knees bending, soles fixed, the same as unbound legs. Each pose is solved with the others at 0, and when they act together they simply add; the feet stay put only when one of them is small. The preset motions therefore never drive crouch and weight shift at the same time.
- **Export requirements**: blend shapes on rotation deformers need Cubism 5.0 or later. With an export target below 5.0, all bone parameters are baked into normal parameter keyforms; poses on rotation deformers are dropped (the export gives a notice).

## Limitations

- **Meshes holding both left and right are not bound.** Limb layers should be split into one left and one right after import with **Model → Split Layers by Mesh…**. If both legs or both feet are still drawn on one layer, automatic inference builds bones for two legs but does not bind that mesh, so no crouch or weight shift is generated; split first, then bind each in **Edit skeleton**.
- **Meshes with hand-edited topology are not densified.** Meshes with manual topology edits in history (subdivide, knife and so on) have those edits replayed by vertex index, so the skeleton inserts no vertices into them. If the bend is not smooth enough, add points at the joint by hand.
- When a flexible chain is very wide on both sides (for example a wide fin), the inner side folds where the bend radius is smaller than the half-width. Reduce the joint limits, or split the wide part into a branch. A sharp bend that already folds back on itself in the drawing (a U shape with a band wider than its turn radius) behaves the same: inner-corner vertices are on the far side of the center of curvature and flip when opening or bending further.
- After straightening, non-flexible child bones under a flexible chain are still skinned by rest geometry and do not unroll with it.
- Meshes on nested warp deformers are limited in precision by the grid spacing: where adjacent unconnected parts are only a few pixels apart at a sharp bend, the drawing's pose may be off by a few pixels; for hairpin bends tighter than the band's half-width (for example a 16px wide U bend with only 4px between), the error can reach a dozen pixels or more.
- Straightening's fit has only as many degrees of freedom as there are bones: when a few bones wrap a large bend (for example three around 200°), the centerline may still ripple by a few pixels after unrolling; subdivide the bones before straightening. Fitting moves joints, the meshes' joint subdivision is recut with them, and vertex counts may change.
- In projects saved by earlier versions, if a regeneration merge never moved the tail mesh over, later merges keep treating it as "reattached by the user" and skeleton changes (including straightening) no longer reach it; the quality report shows `REGENERATION_CONFLICT` with a fixable hint, and running **Update Generated Rig** once restores it.
- Straightened length unrolls by bone length: the curve is slightly shorter than the bone polyline at turns, so the more the drawing bends, the slightly longer it is after unrolling (four bones around 90° are about 3% longer).
- After straightening, hand-painted weights are still sampled at the drawing's positions, but grabbing / painting weights in the editor reads the straightened rest positions, so the two are misaligned; straightened chains are best left on automatic weights.
- Bends beyond about 90° overlap on the inside of the joint, as with Spine's linear skinning; adjust the joint band or split the drawing to improve it.
- After the skeleton is baked, Cubism Editor keeps no bone semantics; to keep editing bones, keep this program's project file.
